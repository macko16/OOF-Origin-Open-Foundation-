About Simulation Purpose & Scope Standard - (SPSS)

Governing Why a Simulation Exists Before Deciding How It Should Represent
Reality

Simulation Exists Before Deciding How It
Should Represent Reality

A simulation may be technically sophisticated and still be unsuitable for the
question it is being used to answer.

Before models, environments, assumptions, scenarios, inputs, or execution
methods are selected, the simulation must establish why it is being performed
and what it is legitimately intended to cover.

Simulation Purpose & Scope Standard (SPSS) provides this governance layer
within SIMULOS®.

Canonical Definition

Simulation Purpose & Scope Standard (SPSS) defines the governance conditions
under which the reason for performing a simulation, the questions it is
intended to address, its governed scope, material inclusions and exclusions,
applicability context, required simulation depth, and appropriate simulation
precision are formally established before downstream simulation representation
begins.

Why SPSS Exists

Organizations use simulations for different purposes: system design,
engineering analysis, safety assessment, operational planning,
autonomous-system testing, AI evaluation, infrastructure planning, scenario
exploration, scientific investigation, training, decision support, and
pre-deployment testing.

These purposes are not interchangeable.

A simulation designed to explore possible behavior may not provide sufficient
basis for a safety-critical decision.

A simulation covering one operating environment cannot automatically support
conclusions about another.

A simulation examining one configuration cannot silently become evidence for
every configuration of the system.

SPSS prevents this purpose and scope inflation.

Governed Space

Simulation Purpose & Scope

SPSS governs the transition from:

Defined Simulation Object

to:

Governed Simulation Inquiry

It establishes what the simulation is expected to investigate before
downstream simulation architecture is constructed around that objective.

Governance Flow

Simulation Object → Simulation Purpose → Simulation Question → Simulation
Scope → Inclusions & Exclusions → Applicability Context → Simulation Depth →
Simulation Precision → Governed Simulation Purpose & Scope

This governed basis then becomes an input to the next SIMULOS® space:

Reality Representation

What SPSS Establishes

A governed simulation should establish:

Purpose — why the simulation is being performed.

Simulation Question — what the simulation is expected to investigate or
demonstrate.

Scope — which parts of the Simulation Object, system, process, environment,
behavior, or interaction are included.

Exclusions — what the simulation explicitly does not represent or evaluate.

Applicability — the environments, configurations, populations, operating
conditions, or contexts to which the simulation relates.

Simulation Depth — how extensively the simulation must investigate the defined
problem.

Simulation Precision — what level of representational and analytical precision
is appropriate for the intended purpose.

Business Value

For organizations using simulation, SPSS creates a clear governance basis
before significant resources are committed to simulation design and execution.

It helps organizations determine whether: the simulation being built actually
addresses the business or engineering question, simulation expenditure is
aligned with the intended decision, critical operating conditions have been
excluded, simulation conclusions are being used beyond their legitimate scope,
different simulation projects are addressing comparable questions, and
downstream teams understand exactly what the simulation is and is not intended
to establish.

This becomes increasingly important when simulation results influence
investment, engineering, deployment, safety, regulatory, autonomous-system, or
high-consequence operational decisions.

Governance Boundary

SPSS begins when a sufficiently identified Simulation Object requires a
defined simulation purpose and governed operational scope.

It ends when the simulation purpose, questions, scope, exclusions,
applicability context, required depth, and appropriate precision have been
sufficiently established to support governed Reality Representation.

SPSS does not determine whether the simulation is valid, whether its evidence
is sufficient for external validation, or whether operational reliance should
ultimately be authorized.

Those questions belong to their appropriate downstream SIMULOS® spaces or
complementary OOF® architectures.

Practical Governance Test

Before simulation development proceeds, an organization should be able to
answer:

What exactly are we trying to learn, test, examine, or demonstrate through
this simulation?

Which reality does that question concern?

What is inside the simulation scope?

What is explicitly outside it?

Under which conditions will the resulting simulation remain applicable?

How much simulation depth and precision does the intended purpose actually
require?

If these questions cannot be answered, downstream simulation sophistication
cannot compensate for the missing governance foundation.

Relationship to SIMULOS®

SPSS is the second Parent Standard of SIMULOS® — Simulation Governance
Architecture.

It follows:

Simulation Object Standard

and precedes:

Reality Representation governance.

Its architectural role is fundamental:

The Simulation Object establishes what is being simulated.

SPSS establishes why it is being simulated and what the simulation is allowed
to cover.

Only after both are established should the architecture begin governing how
relevant reality will be represented.

Foundational Principle

A simulation cannot legitimately answer a broader question than the purpose
and scope for which it was actually designed.

Canonical Closing Statement

Simulation Purpose & Scope Standard establishes the governed inquiry boundary
of SIMULOS® by defining why a simulation is performed, what questions it is
intended to address, what it includes and excludes, where its applicability
begins and ends, and what level of depth and precision its purpose requires.
By establishing these conditions before reality representation, model
construction, scenario design, and execution, SPSS prevents simulation results
from acquiring broader meaning, authority, or applicability than the
simulation was actually designed to support.
